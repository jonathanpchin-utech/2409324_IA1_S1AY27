My reflection on The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason.

Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

The chapter has made me think about how to handle money and prepare for the future. It is saying Someone could earn a lot and still have nothing saved if they spend everything, or as we call it today lifestyle inflation. The advice about saving a tenth of your income and controlling expenses is a practical step people can work on, I think saving that amount could be difficult when money is already tight, but having a plan that you can stick towards is still useful. It also brings up the point about improving your ability to earn. Which in today world mean increasing your skills and marketablity

The Five Laws of Gold

In this Chapter, Nomasir’s experience shows how easily someone can lose money through poor decisions. His father gave him both money and advice, but he only understood the value of that advice after losing the money. I think this is relevant because wanting to make money quickly can make an opportunity seem better than it really is. Before putting money into something, I would want to understand how it works, what could go wrong and whether the person advising me knows what they are talking about.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from both chapters is that I need to learn how to manage the money I earn. Saving consistently, being patient and making informed decisions would help me work towards my plans. Although i do not agree on all the methods forementioned having a plan is always better than moving aimlessly
